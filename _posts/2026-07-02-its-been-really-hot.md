<p>Like the title of this post, the heat and humidity in Pittsburgh have been unbearable. In spite of what I imagine will be a hefty electric bill later this month, I've been blasting the AC unit all day while my poor cat has been sprawled out next to my fan. It's been a very unique experience to be subject to exactly what it is I'm researching in my PhD.</p>

<p>As a quick psa, I'm sure many are already aware, but when managing extreme heat it's important to stay hydrated and replace electrolytes, avoid being outside around peak temperatures, reapply sunscreen every few hours if you're outdoors, wear loose clothing, and, most importantly, be kind to yourself, as extreme heat can worsen sleep quality and productivity.</p>

<p>I figured the heatwave (given my trouble concentrating) would be a good excuse to give a quick series of updates, share a few photos from my summer and travels, and motivate myself to finish the other blog drafts I've been sitting on.</p>

<p>I actually started my summer in Seoul! A childhood friend and I spent two weeks sightseeing, eating good food, and checking out street markets around the city. One highlight was taking a train to Busan for a day trip, and the experience was much more pleasant than in the movie. My experience in Seoul could honestly be a whole separate post. I enjoyed being in such a large city, even if I often stood out as a visibly Black foreigner in certain areas. Most people paid me no mind, and a few people were open and kind in asking where I was visiting from—or, like one woman who spoke to me in rapid-fire Korean, seemed curious about my locs.</p>

<p>Otherwise, I was in DC on two separate policy-related occasions for the AGU Voices for Science program and for the Science Network for Advancing Policy (SNAP) Retreat. Through the former, my group and I were able to visit congressional offices to advocate for STEM researchers, and SNAP was an incredible experience to connect with other early-career researchers stepping into the policy space.</p>
